ⓘ How the grid score works
  • Volatility — 1h / 24h / 7d price movement. More swings means more grid fills.
  • Liquidity — 24h trading volume (log-scaled). Easier fills, less slippage.
  • Turnover — volume ÷ market cap. Rewards healthy, active churn.
  • Trend penalty — a strong 7-day direction lowers the score; grids want a range, not a runaway trend.
The four are combined and normalized 0–100 across the current scan — higher = a better grid candidate, not a price prediction.

FAQ

Questions, answered

How is the grid score calculated?

Each coin's raw score rewards short-term volatility (weighted toward 24h, plus 1h and a daily-ized 7d figure) and liquidity (log-scaled volume plus capped turnover), minus a trend penalty of 2× the absolute 7-day move. Coins under ~$10M daily volume, beyond ±25% over 7d, or that barely move (stablecoins) are filtered out. Survivors are normalized to 0–100.

Why penalize a strong 7-day trend?

Grid bots assume mean reversion. In a strong one-way trend, price walks out of the band, leaving the bot holding losing inventory or fully sold with no upside. Strong trends hurt static grids both ways — the single most important filter.

Is the data live? What if it fails?

Yes — fetched live from CoinGecko's free public API on load and on "Scan now". The free tier is rate-limited and cached ~30–60s, so we don't poll faster. On failure or HTTP 429 we show the last good data with a "cached" badge, or a curated fallback list, clearly labeled.
